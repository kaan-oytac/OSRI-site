For authors

Submission guidelines

What OSRI publishes, what a manuscript must contain, how review works, and the ethics standard every study is measured against. Read this page in full before submitting.

What OSRI publishes

OSRI accepts work from students currently enrolled in a secondary school in the Okanagan Valley (Grades 9–12, or the equivalent in a home-learning program). Work is published in two formats. There are no tiers or rankings; outstanding work is occasionally noted as an editor's selection.

Papers

Length
1,500 to 5,000 words, excluding references, tables, figures, appendices, and acknowledgements. Special exceptions may apply, and contacting OSRI is the best way to see your eligibility.
Abstract
Required, 250 words or fewer
Structure
Introduction, Methodology, Findings, and Discussion in said order for empirical work; a flexible but clearly organized structure for literature reviews
References
Required; at least five for a literature review
Review
Two reviewers plus an editor; ethics review where applicable

Mini-papers

Length
One to three paragraphs, roughly 150 to 500 words
Abstract
Not required
Structure
One claim, the evidence for it, and one limitation. Explainers written for other students are welcome.
References
Required; two at minimum
Review
One editor; ethics review where applicable

Study type

Every submission is labelled with one of three types so readers know what kind of evidence they are looking at:

  • Literature review: synthesizes published sources; no new data.
  • Public dataset study: analyzes data that already exists and is publicly available.
  • Original data: the student produced the data through an experiment, survey, observation or measurement.

Formatting and citations

  • Submit as a shared Google Doc with comment access or as a PDF. A shared Google Doc is preferred.
  • Use APA 7th edition for citations and references. Every claim that is not your own finding needs a citation.
  • Number in ascending order and title every table or figure.
  • Report counts alongside percentages. Report the sample size everywhere a percentage appears.
  • Do not include your name inside the manuscript file. Author details go on the submission form so that review is blind.

Authorship and originality

Each listed author must have contributed materially to the work and be able to defend it. Teachers, parents and mentors who advised but did not write are acknowledged, not listed as authors. Work must not have been published elsewhere, though work previously entered in a science fair or submitted as a Capstone project is welcome.

All submissions are checked for plagiarism. A manuscript found to contain uncredited text or fabricated sources is declined, and the author is notified of the reason.

Use of AI tools

AI tools may be used for language editing, brainstorming, code assistance and literature search, and their use must be disclosed. Every submission includes an AI disclosure statement naming the tool, what it was used for, and how the author verified the output. AI tools may not be listed as authors.

Three uses are not permitted: generating results or data, generating references that the author has not personally read, and writing the Findings or Discussion sections. A fabricated citation can be discerned from genuine errors when citing and will damage the perception of authenticity in your work.

Ethics standard

What OSRI review is, and is not

OSRI ethics review is not Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Research Ethics Board (REB) approval and does not substitute for it. It certifies that a study met OSRI's published standard, which follows the Regeneron ISEF rules for research involving human participants, and that the work is eligible for publication at OSRI. If you intend to submit the same work to a venue that requires REB approval, you must obtain that approval separately.

Studies that use only published sources or public datasets do not need ethics review. Any study that collects data from people is placed into one of three tiers before it is reviewed for content. The tier is determined by the Ethics Review Panel from the study design you describe on the submission form, not by the author.

Green

Panel review. Publishable.

  • Anonymous surveys on non-sensitive topics (study habits, transit use, screen time, food waste)
  • Observational studies of public behaviour in public places
  • Analysis of existing public datasets
  • Interviews with consenting adults on non-sensitive topics

Yellow

Requires a qualified adult supervisor to co-sign the submission before review.

  • Any identifiable data (names, emails, photographs, precise locations)
  • Participants under 13 years of age
  • Audio or video recording
  • Mild deception, which requires a written debrief protocol
  • A power imbalance between researcher and participants: the researcher is the participants' tutor, coach, club president or employer

Red

OSRI does not publish. No exceptions.

  • Mental health screening or anything touching self-harm, suicidality, disordered eating or trauma
  • Substance use or illegal behaviour
  • Medical or clinical interventions, supplements, exercise prescriptions
  • Biological samples of any kind
  • Research on Indigenous communities without engagement with the Nation, following TCPS 2 Chapter 9 and the OCAP® principles (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession)
  • Anything a reasonable parent would be upset to discover after the fact

Consent

Every participant in a green- or yellow-tier study must be told, before taking part, what the study is for, what they will be asked to do, that participation is voluntary, and that they may stop at any time. For participants under 18, a parent or guardian must also give permission unless the study is an anonymous, non-sensitive survey conducted with the school's knowledge. OSRI provides ready-to-use consent templates covering anonymous surveys, parental permission, assent, interviews and debriefs.

Research in schools

OSRI review does not replace your school or district's own permission process. If you plan to survey or observe students at school, obtain permission from the school first and state this on the submission form.

Basis for the standard

The tiers are adapted from the Regeneron International Science and Engineering Fair rules for human participant research, with reference to the Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS 2) that governs research ethics in Canada. The standard is reviewed annually by the Ethics Review Panel. Changes are announced on this site and never applied retroactively to published work.

Review process

  1. Submission check

    An editor confirms the manuscript is complete, within scope, and correctly labelled. Incomplete submissions are returned within one week with a list of what is missing.

  2. Ethics tier

    For studies involving people, the Ethics Review Panel assigns a tier. Yellow-tier studies wait here until the supervisor co-signature arrives. Red-tier studies are declined with a written explanation and, where possible, a suggestion for a safer design.

  3. Content review

    Papers go to two reviewers; mini-papers to one editor. Reviewers assess whether the question is clear, the method fits the question, the evidence supports the claims, and the limitations are stated honestly. Reviewers do not know who the author is.

  4. Decision

    Accept, revise, or decline, with the reviewers' comments attached. Most first decisions are revise. First decisions are sent within four weeks of a complete submission.

  5. Revision

    Authors have six weeks to respond to each round. A short letter explaining what was changed, and what was not and why, accompanies the revised manuscript.

  6. Publication

    The accepted manuscript is copy-edited, the author approves the final version, and the work is published with a permanent link. Papers are also deposited in an archive that issues a DOI.

After publication

Licence. Published work is released under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Authors keep copyright; anyone may share or build on the work with credit.

Corrections. Errors found after publication are corrected with a dated note on the publication page. The original file is kept.

Withdrawal. An author may ask for their work to be withdrawn. The listing remains with a withdrawal notice so that existing links do not break, but the file is removed.

Data appendices. Public dataset studies should cite the exact dataset and version used. Original data studies may attach a de-identified data appendix; no appendix may contain information that could identify a participant.
